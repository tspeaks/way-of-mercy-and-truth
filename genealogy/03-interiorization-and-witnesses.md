# Interiorization and Early Witnesses

## John: from ethics toward interior transformation

The Gospel of John and 1–3 John occupy a transitional position. They do not replace the
ethical nucleus. They deepen it.

The Johannine tradition introduces a stronger vocabulary of abiding, love, truth, inner
transformation, communion, divine life, and the relationship between external conduct and
internal spiritual reality.

This is a decisive genealogical movement:

> ethical obedience → interior transformation

The Way is no longer understood merely as a collection of rules. The objective becomes
the transformation of the person who performs them — a distinction that becomes central
to WMT's understanding of formation.

*In the curriculum:* the whole of Stage Three, Abiding (`WMT S3`), and especially Chapter
Eleven, The Vine (`WMT C11`).

## The early Christian witnesses

The next layer includes **Barnabas, Theophilus, 1 Clement, Diognetus, the Didascalia,**
and the **Pseudo-Clementines**.

These are important not because WMT treats them as a unified theological system, but
because they preserve different echoes of the early movement and demonstrate its
development. They are evidence for both continuity and transformation.

The genealogy therefore moves from historical recovery, to ethical reconstruction, to a
developmental Christian tradition.

*In the curriculum:* Barnabas in Chapter Six (`WMT C06`, with the departure disclosed),
1 Clement in Chapter Fifteen (`WMT C15`), the Pseudo-Clementine fruit test and True
Prophet in Chapter Eight (`WMT C08`).
