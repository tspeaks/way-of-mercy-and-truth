# 2 Chronicles — review package

Scripture: World English Bible (public domain). Index status: complete. Method version 2.3.

Every entry below shows the passage in full, then the claim made about it. Read the passage before the claim. The question is not whether the tags are internally consistent — it is whether they survive contact with the text.

**Note.** Solomon and the kings of Judah to the exile, retold with attention to the temple, reform and repentance. A Tier 6 book, indexed by the Song of Solomon precedent. Not cited by the curriculum or the Scriptural Appendix.

**Prediction note.** Forecast: little beyond the Kings parallels. Outcome: 28:8-15 (Oded and the captives) is a pre-Christian template for the Good Samaritan and one of the strongest narrative anchors for PR06 and PR15 in the Old Testament; 33:12-13 (Manasseh) widens C19 further than any text in Kings.

**Coverage note.** Six entries, by design. Chapters 1-9 parallel 1 Kings and are indexed there; the reigns of the kings of Judah (10-36) are largely parallel to 1-2 Kings and are not re-indexed. Material unique to Chronicles has been checked, and only the passages above anchor formation.

## 1. 2 Chronicles 7:13-14 — If my people, who are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin

> **7:13** “If I shut up the sky so that there is no rain, or if I command the locust to devour the land, or if I send pestilence among my people;
> **7:14** if my people, who are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land.

**Claim.** God's answer to Solomon's dedication prayer, given as a sequence: 'if my people, who are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land.' Four movements — humility, prayer, seeking, turning — and the Failure Recovery Protocol's return given its promise (medium). C19 and C22. The verse is addressed to 'my people,' not to a nation-state.

## 2. 2 Chronicles 20:5-12 — We have no might against this great company that comes against us; neither know we what to do, but our eyes are on you

> **20:5** Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of Yahweh, before the new court;
> **20:6** and he said, “Yahweh, the God of our fathers, aren’t you God in heaven? Aren’t you ruler over all the kingdoms of the nations? Power and might are in your hand, so that no one is able to withstand you.
> **20:7** Didn’t you, our God, drive out the inhabitants of this land before your people Israel, and give it to the seed of Abraham your friend forever?
> **20:8** They lived in it, and have built you a sanctuary in it for your name, saying,
> **20:9** ‘If evil comes on us—the sword, judgment, pestilence, or famine—we will stand before this house, and before you, (for your name is in this house), and cry to you in our affliction, and you will hear and save.’
> **20:10** Now, behold, the children of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom you would not let Israel invade, when they came out of the land of Egypt, but they turned aside from them, and didn’t destroy them;
> **20:11** behold, how they reward us, to come to cast us out of your possession, which you have given us to inherit.
> **20:12** Our God, will you not judge them? For we have no might against this great company that comes against us; neither know we what to do, but our eyes are on you.”

**Claim.** An invasion, and a king who prays in public rather than projecting confidence. He recalls what God has done and names the threat, then ends with an admission: 'Our God, will you not judge them? For we have no might against this great company that comes against us; neither know we what to do, but our eyes are on you.' The Discipline of Unknowing as a confession of incapacity made before the whole assembly (medium). C22 and C09.

## 3. 2 Chronicles 28:8-15 — Aren't there even with you trespasses of your own against Yahweh your God?… took the captives, and with the spoil clothed all who were naked among them… gave them something to eat and to drink, anointed them, carried all the feeble of them on donkeys, and brought them to Jericho

> **28:8** The children of Israel carried away captive of their brothers two hundred thousand, women, sons, and daughters, and took also away much spoil from them, and brought the spoil to Samaria.
> **28:9** But a prophet of Yahweh was there, whose name was Oded: and he went out to meet the army that came to Samaria, and said to them, “Behold, because Yahweh, the God of your fathers, was angry with Judah, he has delivered them into your hand, and you have slain them in a rage which has reached up to heaven.
> **28:10** Now you purpose to keep under the children of Judah and Jerusalem for bondservants and bondmaids for yourselves. Aren’t there even with you trespasses of your own against Yahweh your God?
> **28:11** Now hear me therefore, and send back the captives, that you have taken captive from your brothers; for the fierce wrath of Yahweh is on you.”
> **28:12** Then some of the heads of the children of Ephraim, Azariah the son of Johanan, Berechiah the son of Meshillemoth, and Jehizkiah the son of Shallum, and Amasa the son of Hadlai, stood up against those who came from the war,
> **28:13** and said to them, “You shall not bring in the captives here: for you purpose that which will bring on us a trespass against Yahweh, to add to our sins and to our trespass; for our trespass is great, and there is fierce wrath against Israel.”
> **28:14** So the armed men left the captives and the spoil before the princes and all the assembly.
> **28:15** The men who have been mentioned by name rose up, and took the captives, and with the spoil clothed all who were naked among them, dressed them, gave them sandals, and gave them something to eat and to drink, anointed them, carried all the feeble of them on donkeys, and brought them to Jericho, the city of palm trees, to their brothers. Then they returned to Samaria.

**Claim.** The northern army returns from war with two hundred thousand captives from Judah. A prophet named Oded meets them at the gate: 'you have slain them in a rage which has reached up to heaven. Now you purpose to keep under the children of Judah… for bondservants… Aren't there even with you trespasses of your own against Yahweh your God?' Self-Accusation turned on a victorious army (medium). Four named leaders stand with him, and the soldiers give up their captives. Then the response: 'The men who have been mentioned by name rose up, and took the captives, and with the spoil clothed all who were naked among them, dressed them, gave them sandals, and gave them something to eat and to drink, anointed them, carried all the feeble of them on donkeys, and brought them to Jericho, the city of palm trees, to their brothers.' Non-Retaliation and Shedding Harshness carried out on the enemy's wounded. Clothed, fed, anointed, put on donkeys, brought to Jericho — the Good Samaritan's actions, in the same order and on the same road, in a text Jesus's hearers knew. C03, C27 and C04.

## 4. 2 Chronicles 30:18-20 — May the good Yahweh pardon everyone who sets his heart to seek God, Yahweh, the God of his fathers, even if they aren't clean according to the purification of the sanctuary. Yahweh listened to Hezekiah, and healed the people

> **30:18** For a multitude of the people, even many of Ephraim and Manasseh, Issachar and Zebulun, had not cleansed themselves, yet they ate the Passover otherwise than it is written. For Hezekiah had prayed for them, saying, “May the good Yahweh pardon everyone
> **30:19** who sets his heart to seek God, Yahweh, the God of his fathers, even if they aren’t clean according to the purification of the sanctuary.”
> **30:20** Yahweh listened to Hezekiah, and healed the people.

**Claim.** At Hezekiah's Passover, many who came from the north had not been purified and 'ate the Passover otherwise than it is written.' Hezekiah does not exclude them; he prays: 'May the good Yahweh pardon everyone who sets his heart to seek God… even if they aren't clean according to the purification of the sanctuary. Yahweh listened to Hezekiah, and healed the people.' C06's reading of the rule by the heart it serves, and C19's door held open for the ones who came imperfectly.

## 5. 2 Chronicles 33:10-13 — When he was in distress, he begged Yahweh his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers… and he was entreated by him… Then Manasseh knew that Yahweh was God

> **33:10** Yahweh spoke to Manasseh, and to his people; but they gave no heed.
> **33:11** Therefore Yahweh brought on them the captains of the army of the king of Assyria, who took Manasseh in chains, and bound him with fetters, and carried him to Babylon.
> **33:12** When he was in distress, he begged Yahweh his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers.
> **33:13** He prayed to him; and he was entreated by him, and heard his supplication, and brought him again to Jerusalem into his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that Yahweh was God.

**Claim.** Manasseh is the worst king in the history of Judah — 2 Kings 21 blames the exile on him. Chronicles adds what happened after his capture: 'When he was in distress, he begged Yahweh his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers. He prayed to him; and he was entreated by him, and heard his supplication, and brought him again to Jerusalem into his kingdom.' C19's door at its widest — even for him — and the Failure Recovery Protocol's return in chains (medium). C22. 'Then Manasseh knew that Yahweh was God.'

## 6. 2 Chronicles 36:15-16 — Yahweh… sent to them by his messengers, rising up early and sending, because he had compassion on his people… but they mocked the messengers of God… until there was no remedy

> **36:15** Yahweh, the God of their fathers, sent to them by his messengers, rising up early and sending, because he had compassion on his people, and on his dwelling place:
> **36:16** but they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and scoffed at his prophets, until the wrath of Yahweh arose against his people, until there was no remedy.

**Claim.** The Chronicler's summary of why Jerusalem fell: 'Yahweh, the God of their fathers, sent to them by his messengers, rising up early and sending, because he had compassion on his people, and on his dwelling place: but they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and scoffed at his prophets, until the wrath of Yahweh arose against his people, until there was no remedy.' C08's true prophets refused, and C19's compassion — 'rising up early and sending' — outlasted until it could do no more.
