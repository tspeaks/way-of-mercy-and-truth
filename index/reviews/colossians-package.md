# Colossians — review package

Scripture: World English Bible (public domain). Index status: complete — 81 of 95 verses; the greeting (1:1-2) and the closing greetings (4:7-18) are unindexed; awaiting second-pass review. Method version 2.3.

Every entry below shows the passage in full, then the claim made about it. Read the passage before the claim. The question is not whether the tags are internally consistent — it is whether they survive contact with the text.

**Note.** A letter against a religion of rules, written to a church Paul had never visited. Two entries carry prior citations — 2:3 from C02 and 3:3 from C23 — and serve as the calibration set. The indexing order expected C23 from 'hidden with Christ,' and C23 is reached twice: by the prior at 3:1-4, and with its practice at 3:22-4:1.

**Coverage note.** The closing greetings (4:7-18) are left out, though two names in them carry weight in other books: Onesimus, 'the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you,' is the runaway slave of Philemon, and Mark, who is to be received, is the man Paul refused to take on his second journey (Acts 15:37-39). Both are plausible anchors for PR29, Merciful Reintegration — but only with Philemon and Acts in hand, since Colossians itself never says either man had failed. They are recorded here for whoever indexes those books, and not tagged here, because an anchor should rest on what the indexed text says. No entry reaches PR24, PR27, or PR28; 3:5-11 and 3:12-15 come nearest to PR24 and PR27 respectively, and both are declined in their entries with the reason.

## 1. Colossians 1:3-8 — Bearing fruit and growing, as it does in you also

> **1:3** We give thanks to God the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, praying always for you,
> **1:4** having heard of your faith in Christ Jesus, and of the love which you have toward all the saints,
> **1:5** because of the hope which is laid up for you in the heavens, of which you heard before in the word of the truth of the Good News,
> **1:6** which has come to you; even as it is in all the world and is bearing fruit and growing, as it does in you also, since the day you heard and knew the grace of God in truth;
> **1:7** even as you learned of Epaphras our beloved fellow servant, who is a faithful servant of Christ on our behalf,
> **1:8** who also declared to us your love in the Spirit.

**Claim.** What Paul has heard of them is love 'toward all the saints,' and he treats it as the evidence of their faith — C12's proof. The gospel is described as something that bears fruit and grows, in the world and in them, from a day of first hearing onward: growth with a beginning and no stated end, which is C25's shape.

## 2. Colossians 1:9-14 — Filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom

> **1:9** For this cause, we also, since the day we heard this, don’t cease praying and making requests for you, that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,
> **1:10** that you may walk worthily of the Lord, to please him in all respects, bearing fruit in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God;
> **1:11** strengthened with all power, according to the might of his glory, for all endurance and perseverance with joy;
> **1:12** giving thanks to the Father, who made us fit to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light;
> **1:13** who delivered us out of the power of darkness, and translated us into the Kingdom of the Son of his love;
> **1:14** in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins;

**Claim.** Wisdom and understanding are prayed for in order that they may issue in a walk — 'bearing fruit in every good work' — and in endurance held 'with joy.' C02's claim that wisdom is a skill of living rather than an intelligence, and C12's that knowledge which bears no fruit is not yet knowledge.

## 3. Colossians 1:15-20 — In him all things are held together

> **1:15** who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.
> **1:16** For by him all things were created, in the heavens and on the earth, things visible and things invisible, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or powers; all things have been created through him, and for him.
> **1:17** He is before all things, and in him all things are held together.
> **1:18** He is the head of the body, the assembly, who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all things he might have the preeminence.
> **1:19** For all the fullness was pleased to dwell in him;
> **1:20** and through him to reconcile all things to himself, by him, whether things on the earth, or things in the heavens, having made peace through the blood of his cross.

**Claim.** All things, visible and invisible, created through him and for him, and held together in him. C01 opens the curriculum with the claim that reality has a grain — that the moral order and the character of God are one thing — and this hymn gives that grain a name. The scale of the claim, thrones and dominions included, is the landscape C24 asks the reader to stand before in wonder rather than analysis.

## 4. Colossians 1:21-23 — Alienated and enemies in your mind, yet now reconciled

> **1:21** You, being in past times alienated and enemies in your mind in your evil works,
> **1:22** yet now he has reconciled in the body of his flesh through death, to present you holy and without blemish and blameless before him,
> **1:23** if it is so that you continue in the faith, grounded and steadfast, and not moved away from the hope of the Good News which you heard, which is being proclaimed in all creation under heaven; of which I, Paul, was made a servant.

**Claim.** The former condition is stated without softening — alienated, enemies 'in your mind,' in evil works — and the change is stated as already accomplished by another. C19's door: the hope comes first, and the condition attached is continuance, 'grounded and steadfast,' not prior worthiness.

## 5. Colossians 1:24-29 — Christ in you, the hope of glory

> **1:24** Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and fill up on my part that which is lacking of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh for his body’s sake, which is the assembly;
> **1:25** of which I was made a servant, according to the stewardship of God which was given me toward you, to fulfill the word of God,
> **1:26** the mystery which has been hidden for ages and generations. But now it has been revealed to his saints,
> **1:27** to whom God was pleased to make known what are the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory;
> **1:28** whom we proclaim, admonishing every man and teaching every man in all wisdom, that we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus;
> **1:29** for which I also labor, striving according to his working, which works in me mightily.

**Claim.** The centre of the mystery is an indwelling — 'Christ in you' — and the goal is to present every person complete. That is C26's change of nature from within. The apostle describes his part as stewardship and labour 'according to his working, which works in me mightily,' service done by a power not his own, which is C16's account of what authority is for.

## 6. Colossians 2:1-5 — In whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden

> **2:1** For I desire to have you know how greatly I struggle for you, and for those at Laodicea, and for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh;
> **2:2** that their hearts may be comforted, they being knit together in love, and gaining all riches of the full assurance of understanding, that they may know the mystery of God, both of the Father and of Christ,
> **2:3** in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden.
> **2:4** Now this I say that no one may delude you with persuasiveness of speech.
> **2:5** For though I am absent in the flesh, yet am I with you in the spirit, rejoicing and seeing your order, and the steadfastness of your faith in Christ.

**Claim.** C02 quotes 2:3 directly: the treasures of wisdom are located in a person rather than a system. The sentence that follows gives the reason for saying so — 'that no one may delude you with persuasiveness of speech' — and the test against persuasiveness is C08's.

## 7. Colossians 2:6-7 — Rooted and built up in him

> **2:6** As therefore you received Christ Jesus, the Lord, walk in him,
> **2:7** rooted and built up in him, and established in the faith, even as you were taught, abounding in it in thanksgiving.

**Claim.** Walk in him as you received him, rooted and built up: the life continues by the same means it began, which is C11's argument that abiding is the mechanism rather than a stage to graduate from. The paragraph ends 'abounding in thanksgiving,' which is the practice C10 assigns to the delight of remaining.

## 8. Colossians 2:8-15 — A circumcision not made with hands

> **2:8** Be careful that you don’t let anyone rob you through his philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the elements of the world, and not after Christ.
> **2:9** For in him all the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily,
> **2:10** and in him you are made full, who is the head of all principality and power;
> **2:11** in whom you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made with hands, in the putting off of the body of the sins of the flesh, in the circumcision of Christ;
> **2:12** having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith in the working of God, who raised him from the dead.
> **2:13** You were dead through your trespasses and the uncircumcision of your flesh. He made you alive together with him, having forgiven us all our trespasses,
> **2:14** wiping out the handwriting in ordinances which was against us; and he has taken it out of the way, nailing it to the cross;
> **2:15** having stripped the principalities and the powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

**Claim.** Three moves in one paragraph. Philosophy 'after the tradition of men' is to be tested, as C08 tests every teacher. The outward sign is read inwardly — circumcision not made with hands — which is the reading C06 takes from Jeremiah 4:4. And the record against us is not argued down but cancelled, 'nailing it to the cross,' which is the most concrete image in the Pauline letters of C19's open door.

**Tags.** pillars: truth, mercy · stages: S2, S5 · chapters: C06, C19, C08 · practices: none · confidence: high

## 9. Colossians 2:16-19 — A shadow of the things to come; but the body is Christ's

> **2:16** Let no one therefore judge you in eating, or in drinking, or with respect to a feast day or a new moon or a Sabbath day,
> **2:17** which are a shadow of the things to come; but the body is Christ’s.
> **2:18** Let no one rob you of your prize by a voluntary humility and worshipping of the angels, dwelling in the things which he has not seen, vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind,
> **2:19** and not holding firmly to the Head, from whom all the body, being supplied and knit together through the joints and ligaments, grows with God’s growth.

**Claim.** Feasts, new moons, and sabbaths are called 'a shadow of the things to come' — the same interpretive key C06 takes from Hebrews 10:1, where a shadow implies something casting it. The body, by contrast, 'grows with God's growth,' supplied and knit together from the Head: growth that is received rather than manufactured, C25's picture.

## 10. Colossians 2:20-23 — Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch

> **2:20** If you died with Christ from the elements of the world, why, as though living in the world, do you subject yourselves to ordinances,
> **2:21** “Don’t handle, nor taste, nor touch”
> **2:22** (all of which perish with use), according to the precepts and doctrines of men?
> **2:23** Which things indeed appear like wisdom in self-imposed worship, and humility, and severity to the body; but aren’t of any value against the indulgence of the flesh.

**Claim.** Ascetic rules are conceded their appearance — 'like wisdom… and humility, and severity to the body' — and denied their effect: they 'aren't of any value against the indulgence of the flesh.' This is the strongest scriptural statement of the claim C26 and C09 both make, that suppressing a desire does not change it and that holiness comes by acquiring a new want rather than policing the old one. PR20 shares the subject of religious severity but is defined by a guest's arrival, which is absent here.

## 11. Colossians 3:1-4 — Your life is hidden with Christ in God

> **3:1** If then you were raised together with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated on the right hand of God.
> **3:2** Set your mind on the things that are above, not on the things that are on the earth.
> **3:3** For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.
> **3:4** When Christ, our life, is revealed, then you will also be revealed with him in glory.

**Claim.** C23 cites 3:3 to make the point that hiddenness is not a limitation of the arrangement but the arrangement itself. Set the mind on things above, because the life is already hidden and will only later be revealed: C18's loosened grip on things on the earth, grounded in where the life actually is. PR13 is not tagged. The passage describes where the life is kept, not a practice of secret good done unwitnessed; the curriculum uses it at chapter level and the practice anchor belongs to passages like Colossians 3:22-24.

## 12. Colossians 3:5-11 — Put off the old man… where there can't be Greek and Jew

> **3:5** Put to death therefore your members which are on the earth: sexual immorality, uncleanness, depraved passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry;
> **3:6** for which things’ sake the wrath of God comes on the children of disobedience.
> **3:7** You also once walked in those, when you lived in them;
> **3:8** but now you also put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and shameful speaking out of your mouth.
> **3:9** Don’t lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old man with his doings,
> **3:10** and have put on the new man, who is being renewed in knowledge after the image of his Creator,
> **3:11** where there can’t be Greek and Jew, circumcision and uncircumcision, barbarian, Scythian, bondservant, freeman; but Christ is all, and in all.

**Claim.** Two lists of what is put to death and put away — the desires, and then the sins of speech: anger, malice, slander, lying — and a new self 'being renewed in knowledge after the image of his Creator.' Renewal as a continuing process, C26's. The renewal is then described as erasing every line by which people rank each other, down to the Scythian, the byword for the barbarian: C27's circle with no one standing outside. PR15 and PR24 were considered. Both share the image; neither is prescribed — the passage states the unity of the new humanity as a fact rather than commanding the widening or the walk toward the centre.

## 13. Colossians 3:12-15 — Bearing with one another, and forgiving each other

> **3:12** Put on therefore, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, a heart of compassion, kindness, lowliness, humility, and perseverance;
> **3:13** bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, if any man has a complaint against any; even as Christ forgave you, so you also do.
> **3:14** Above all these things, walk in love, which is the bond of perfection.
> **3:15** And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to which also you were called in one body; and be thankful.

**Claim.** Virtues to be put on like clothing, then their use in friction: bearing with one another and forgiving 'if any man has a complaint against any,' as Christ forgave. The peace of God is to 'rule' in the heart — the verb is the umpire's — because they were called into one body: conflict aimed at the body's repair, which is the Architecture of Peace. Love as 'the bond of perfection' is C29's fully formed heart. PR27 is the nearest it has yet come — a list of virtues and a friction in view — but the passage prescribes the response to the friction, not the practice of reading each friction as the material for a particular virtue, and it is not tagged.

**Tags.** mode: prescribes (the passage commands this practice) · pillars: mercy, humility · stages: S4, S7 · chapters: C15, C29 · practices: PR09 · confidence: medium

## 14. Colossians 3:16-17 — Whatever you do, in word or in deed, do all in the name of the Lord

> **3:16** Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly; in all wisdom teaching and admonishing one another with psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your heart to the Lord.
> **3:17** Whatever you do, in word or in deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father, through him.

**Claim.** The scope is everything — 'whatever you do, in word or in deed' — and the instruction is to do it in the name of the Lord, with thanks. That is the Sacrament of the Ordinary as a rule of life: no location exempt, the ordinary as the only place the road is walked, which is C28's liturgy. The singing and thanksgiving carry C10's gratitude.

## 15. Colossians 3:18-21 — Fathers, don't provoke your children, so that they won't be discouraged

> **3:18** Wives, be in subjection to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord.
> **3:19** Husbands, love your wives, and don’t be bitter against them.
> **3:20** Children, obey your parents in all things, for this pleases the Lord.
> **3:21** Fathers, don’t provoke your children, so that they won’t be discouraged.

**Claim.** The household code in its shortest form. What is distinctive is that each command to the stronger party is aimed at the weaker party's inner state: husbands are not to be bitter, and fathers are not to provoke their children 'so that they won't be discouraged.' C14's claim that the household is where formation is tested, with the father's conduct measured by what it does to a child's spirit. The instruction to wives sits inside a first-century household code; the index anchors what the passage asks of those who hold power in the home, and the curriculum's guard against loyalty used to keep someone in harm applies.

## 16. Colossians 3:22-4:1 — Not just when they are looking, as men pleasers

> **3:22** Servants, obey in all things those who are your masters according to the flesh, not just when they are looking, as men pleasers, but in singleness of heart, fearing God.
> **3:23** And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord, and not for men,
> **3:24** knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ.
> **3:25** But he who does wrong will receive again for the wrong that he has done, and there is no partiality.
> **4:1** Masters, give to your servants that which is just and equal, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.

**Claim.** Addressed to household slaves, whose situation the index does not commend, the passage names the exact temptation the Hidden Life is for: work done 'when they are looking, as men pleasers.' Against it — whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men. That is PR13's question stated as a command (what would I do if no one would ever find out?) and PR23's claim that the highest form of the road is honest work in the least glamorous setting. Masters are then held to what is 'just and equal' under a Master of their own, which keeps the passage from being read as a charter for the household's power.

## 17. Colossians 4:2-4 — Continue steadfastly in prayer, watching therein with thanksgiving

> **4:2** Continue steadfastly in prayer, watching therein with thanksgiving;
> **4:3** praying together for us also, that God may open to us a door for the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in bonds;
> **4:4** that I may reveal it as I ought to speak.

**Claim.** Prayer described as something continued in rather than performed, with watchfulness inside it. C09's secret place and C20's watchman joined in a single verb phrase. PR30 shares the subject of continuity in prayer but is defined as the Lord's Prayer three times a day, which this does not prescribe; PR12 shares the word 'watching,' but the object here is prayer rather than the movements of the heart, so neither is tagged.

## 18. Colossians 4:5-6 — Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside

> **4:5** Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time.
> **4:6** Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one.

**Claim.** Conduct toward outsiders is a matter of wisdom and of time — 'redeeming the time' — and speech is to be gracious, seasoned, and fitted to each person: 'how you ought to answer each one.' C17's public square, and C02's wisdom as a practical skill exercised on particular people rather than on audiences.
