1 Peter 1:3-9

Though now you don't see him, you rejoice

3Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to his great mercy became our father again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,

4to an incorruptible and undefiled inheritance that doesn’t fade away, reserved in Heaven for you,

5who by the power of God are guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.

6Wherein you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while, if need be, you have been put to grief in various trials,

7that the proof of your faith, which is more precious than gold that perishes even though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise, glory, and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ—

8whom not having known you love; in whom, though now you don’t see him, yet believing, you rejoice greatly with joy unspeakable and full of glory—

9receiving the result of your faith, the salvation of your souls.

The letter opens on hope before it says a word about conduct, and the hope is set inside grief rather than after it: 'for a little while… you have been put to grief in various trials.' Joy is attributed to people who cannot see what they love. C19 begins with hope deliberately for the same reason, and C10 makes the same claim that joy is a consequence of where one stands, not of what one currently feels.

1 Peter 1:10-12

The prophets searched; angels desire to look

10Concerning this salvation, the prophets sought and searched diligently, who prophesied of the grace that would come to you,

11searching for who or what kind of time the Spirit of Christ, which was in them, pointed to, when he predicted the sufferings of Christ, and the glories that would follow them.

12To them it was revealed, that not to themselves, but to you, they ministered these things, which now have been announced to you through those who preached the Good News to you by the Holy Spirit sent out from heaven; which things angels desire to look into.

The prophets are described as ministering a message whose time and subject they were still searching for — 'not to themselves, but to you.' This is the reading habit C06 teaches: the older text points past itself, and its first speakers did not hold the whole of what they carried. It shares subject matter with the Discipline of Unknowing but prescribes no posture to the reader, so PR04 is not tagged.

1 Peter 1:13-16

Prepare your minds for action; be sober

13Therefore, prepare your minds for action, be sober and set your hope fully on the grace that will be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ—

14as children of obedience, not conforming yourselves according to your former lusts as in your ignorance,

15but just as he who called you is holy, you yourselves also be holy in all of your behavior;

16because it is written, “You shall be holy; for I am holy.”

'Be sober' is the verb from which the desert writers took nepsis, the name of the practice itself, and here it is joined to a specific object: 'not conforming yourselves according to your former lusts.' Readiness of mind against the old desires before they are acted on is Watchfulness in its source form. The holiness command that follows gives the reason for the attention, not a separate instruction.

1 Peter 1:17-21

Pass the time of your living as foreigners here

17If you call on him as Father, who without respect of persons judges according to each man’s work, pass the time of your living as foreigners here in reverent fear:

18knowing that you were redeemed, not with corruptible things, with silver or gold, from the useless way of life handed down from your fathers,

19but with precious blood, as of a faultless and pure lamb, the blood of Christ;

20who was foreknown indeed before the foundation of the world, but was revealed at the end of times for your sake,

21who through him are believers in God, who raised him from the dead, and gave him glory; so that your faith and hope might be in God.

The address of the letter — foreigners in the Dispersion — becomes an instruction: live out the stay as foreigners, in reverent fear. The ground given is a redemption 'not with corruptible things, with silver or gold, from the useless way of life handed down from your fathers.' Residence without ultimate belonging (C17) and the loosening of what silver and gold can purchase (C18) are given one argument.

1 Peter 1:22-25

All flesh is like grass; the word remains forever

22Seeing you have purified your souls in your obedience to the truth through the Spirit in sincere brotherly affection, love one another from the heart fervently:

23having been born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, through the word of God, which lives and remains forever.

24For, “All flesh is like grass, and all of man’s glory like the flower in the grass. The grass withers, and its flower falls;

25but the Lord’s word endures forever.” This is the word of Good News which was preached to you.

Purification is measured by its outcome, 'sincere brotherly affection,' and the command is to love 'from the heart fervently' — the inner work proved by what it produces between people, which is the argument of C12. The Isaiah quotation sets that love against the transience of all human glory, which is the comparison C18 makes when it asks what is worth holding.

1 Peter 2:4-10

Who had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy

4coming to him, a living stone, rejected indeed by men, but chosen by God, precious.

5You also, as living stones, are built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

6Because it is contained in Scripture, “Behold, I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone, chosen, and precious: He who believes in him will not be disappointed.”

7For you who believe therefore is the honor, but for those who are disobedient, “The stone which the builders rejected, has become the chief cornerstone,”

1 Peter 2:11-12

Foreigners and pilgrims, by your good works which they see

11Beloved, I beg you as foreigners and pilgrims, to abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul;

12having good behavior among the nations, so in that of which they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they see, glorify God in the day of visitation.

C17 quotes these two verses in full as the pattern of the chapter. The mechanism is neither argument nor power but conduct observed by people who are speaking against the community — full participation in a society that suspects you, while the loyalty that shapes the conduct is held elsewhere. That is the Citizen-Stranger's definition stated as a command.

1 Peter 2:13-17

As free, and not using your freedom for a cloak

13Therefore subject yourselves to every ordinance of man for the Lord’s sake: whether to the king, as supreme;

14or to governors, as sent by him for vengeance on evildoers and for praise to those who do well.

16as free, and not using your freedom for a cloak of wickedness, but as bondservants of God.

Submission to human ordinance is grounded 'for the Lord's sake,' and immediately qualified: 'as free… as bondservants of God.' 1 Peter 2:18-25

When he was cursed, he didn't curse back

18Servants, be in subjection to your masters with all fear; not only to the good and gentle, but also to the wicked.

19For it is commendable if someone endures pain, suffering unjustly, because of conscience toward God.

20For what glory is it if, when you sin, you patiently endure beating? But if, when you do well, you patiently endure suffering, this is commendable with God.

21For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps,

22who did not sin, “neither was deceit found in his mouth.”

23Who, when he was cursed, didn’t curse back. When he suffered, didn’t threaten, but committed himself to him who judges righteously;

24who his own self bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live to righteousness; by whose stripes you were healed.

25For you were going astray like sheep; but now have returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.

The instruction is addressed to household slaves with no legal exit from harsh masters, and the index does not lift it into counsel to remain under abuse; the same guard the curriculum attaches to PR26 applies here. What it anchors is the pattern in 2:21-23, offered as the example to follow: cursed, he did not curse back; suffering, he did not threaten, but handed judgment to God. That is Non-Retaliation in action and in speech, with its interior mechanism named. The closing image, sheep returned to the Shepherd, is the image C19 opens with.

1 Peter 3:1-6

The hidden person of the heart

1In the same way, wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; so that, even if any don’t obey the Word, they may be won by the behavior of their wives without a word;

2seeing your pure behavior in fear.

3Let your beauty be not just the outward adorning of braiding the hair, and of wearing jewels of gold, or of putting on fine clothing;

4but in the hidden person of the heart, in the incorruptible adornment of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God very precious.

5For this is how the holy women before, who hoped in God also adorned themselves, being in subjection to their own husbands:

6as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, whose children you now are, if you do well, and are not put in fear by any terror.

C23 cites 3:4 directly: value located in the hidden person of the heart, the incorruptible adornment of a gentle and quiet spirit, precious in the sight of God. The surrounding verses describe a conduct that wins 'without a word' — the same mechanism as 2:12, made unwitnessed and unargued. The instruction to wives sits inside a first-century household code; the index anchors the location of value in the unseen, not the code's ordering of the household, and the PR26 guard applies here as well.

1 Peter 3:7

Joint heirs of the grace of life

7You husbands, in the same way, live with your wives according to knowledge, giving honor to the woman, as to the weaker vessel, as being also joint heirs of the grace of life; that your prayers may not be hindered.

The husband is told to live with his wife 'according to knowledge,' giving honour, as a co-heir rather than a subordinate, and the reason given is that his prayers may otherwise be hindered. C14's claim is that the household is where formation is tested, and this verse makes domestic conduct a condition of the interior life rather than a separate department of it.

Not rendering evil for evil, or insult for insult, but blessing

1 Peter 3:13-17

Ready to give an answer, with humility and fear

13Now who is he who will harm you, if you become imitators of that which is good?

14But even if you should suffer for righteousness’ sake, you are blessed. “Don’t fear what they fear, neither be troubled.”

15But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts; and always be ready to give an answer to everyone who asks you a reason concerning the hope that is in you, with humility and fear:

16having a good conscience; that, while you are spoken against as evildoers, they may be disappointed who curse your good way of life in Christ.

17For it is better, if it is God’s will, that you suffer for doing well than for doing evil.

The defence of hope is to be given to anyone who asks, but its manner is specified — humility, fear, a good conscience — so that those who slander are put to shame by the life rather than by the argument. C17's public-square posture. The practice tag is carried by 2:11-12 and 2:13-17, which state it more directly; repeating it here would add a count rather than an anchor.

1 Peter 3:18-22

The righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you to God

18Because Christ also suffered for sins once, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring you to God; being put to death in the flesh, but made alive in the spirit;

19in which he also went and preached to the spirits in prison,

20who before were disobedient, when God waited patiently in the days of Noah, while the ship was being built. In it, few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.

21This is a symbol of baptism, which now saves you—not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

22who is at the right hand of God, having gone into heaven, angels and authorities and powers being made subject to him.

The centre of the paragraph is the purpose clause — the righteous for the unrighteous, 'that he might bring you to God' — which is C19's door. Baptism is then defined against its outward reading: not the washing of the flesh but the answer of a good conscience toward God, the inward reading of an outward sign that C06 teaches. The preaching to the spirits in prison is among the most disputed verses in the New Testament and is left uninterpreted here.

1 Peter 4:7-11

Be hospitable to one another without grumbling

7But the end of all things is near. Therefore be of sound mind, self-controlled, and sober in prayer.

8And above all things be earnest in your love among yourselves, for love covers a multitude of sins.

9Be hospitable to one another without grumbling.

10As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another, as good managers of the grace of God in its various forms.

11If anyone speaks, let it be as it were the very words of God. If anyone serves, let it be as of the strength which God supplies, that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom belong the glory and the dominion forever and ever. Amen.

Hospitality is commanded with its characteristic failure named — grumbling — and gifts are to be used 'as good managers of the grace of God,' held for others rather than owned. C16's service and C14's household open door. Two tags were considered and declined. 'Love covers a multitude of sins' shares PR17's subject but describes what love does, not the act of shielding someone from exposure. Hospitality without grumbling shares PR20's subject, but nothing here breaks a routine for a guest; the grumbling is the host's, not a peevish guest's, so PR22 does not apply either.

1 Peter 4:12-19

Don't be astonished at the fiery trial

12Beloved, don’t be astonished at the fiery trial which has come upon you, to test you, as though a strange thing happened to you.

13But because you are partakers of Christ’s sufferings, rejoice; that at the revelation of his glory you also may rejoice with exceeding joy.

14If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed; because the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you. On their part he is blasphemed, but on your part he is glorified.

15For let none of you suffer as a murderer, or a thief, or an evil doer, or a meddler in other men’s matters.

16But if one of you suffers for being a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God in this matter.

17For the time has come for judgment to begin with the household of God. If it begins first with us, what will happen to those who don’t obey the Good News of God?

18“If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will happen to the ungodly and the sinner?”

19Therefore let them also who suffer according to the will of God in doing good entrust their souls to him, as to a faithful Creator.

C14 cites 4:19 directly, and the Scriptural Appendix names this passage as the ground of Unreciprocated Care: keep doing good while it hurts and entrust the soul to a faithful Creator. The drafter notes the attractor test without overriding the author: the passage is about being insulted for doing good, and PR22 is defined more narrowly as bearing with ungrateful behaviour from those one is helping. The overlap is real — doing good to people who answer it with insult — but the recipients of the good are not named as the ones insulting. The author's placement stands; a reviewer who disagrees is disagreeing with WMT A3.

1 Peter 5:1-4

Not lording it over those entrusted to you

1I exhort the elders among you, as a fellow elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and who will also share in the glory that will be revealed.

2Shepherd the flock of God which is among you, exercising the oversight, not under compulsion, but voluntarily, not for dishonest gain, but willingly;

3neither as lording it over those entrusted to you, but making yourselves examples to the flock.

4When the chief Shepherd is revealed, you will receive the crown of glory that doesn’t fade away.

Three paired contrasts for those who hold authority — not under compulsion but willingly, not for gain but eagerly, not lording it but as examples — written by a man who calls himself a fellow elder rather than a superior. C16 opens by saying authority corrupts nearly everyone who holds it; this is the apostolic rule against each of the three corruptions.

1 Peter 5:5-7

Clothe yourselves with humility; cast all your worries on him

5Likewise, you younger ones, be subject to the elder. Yes, all of you clothe yourselves with humility, to subject yourselves to one another; for “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”

6Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time;

7casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.

Humility is commanded toward one another before it is commanded toward God, and it is pictured as something put on — the verb is the one for tying on a servant's apron. The anxiety that follows is not suppressed but handed over 'because he cares for you,' which is C18's freedom from what one is grasping, and the posture of the weaned child in C22's psalm.

1 Peter 5:8-11

Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful

8Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful. Your adversary, the devil, walks around like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.

9Withstand him steadfast in your faith, knowing that your brothers who are in the world are undergoing the same sufferings.

10But may the God of all grace, who called you to his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after you have suffered a little while, perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you.

11To him be the glory and the power forever and ever. Amen.

The two verbs of the desert watchman in one line — be sober, be watchful — with the danger pictured as something circling rather than something already inside. The counsel is steadiness ('withstand him steadfast') and solidarity with others under the same pressure, not anxious self-scrutiny, which is the tone C20 insists on. The promise that follows is restoration after 'a little while,' not exemption.
