Colossians 1:3-8

Bearing fruit and growing, as it does in you also

3We give thanks to God the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, praying always for you,

4having heard of your faith in Christ Jesus, and of the love which you have toward all the saints,

5because of the hope which is laid up for you in the heavens, of which you heard before in the word of the truth of the Good News,

6which has come to you; even as it is in all the world and is bearing fruit and growing, as it does in you also, since the day you heard and knew the grace of God in truth;

7even as you learned of Epaphras our beloved fellow servant, who is a faithful servant of Christ on our behalf,

8who also declared to us your love in the Spirit.

What Paul has heard of them is love 'toward all the saints,' and he treats it as the evidence of their faith — C12's proof. The gospel is described as something that bears fruit and grows, in the world and in them, from a day of first hearing onward: growth with a beginning and no stated end, which is C25's shape.

Christ in you, the hope of glory

24Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and fill up on my part that which is lacking of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh for his body’s sake, which is the assembly;

25of which I was made a servant, according to the stewardship of God which was given me toward you, to fulfill the word of God,

26the mystery which has been hidden for ages and generations. But now it has been revealed to his saints,

Colossians 2:8-15

A circumcision not made with hands

8Be careful that you don’t let anyone rob you through his philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the elements of the world, and not after Christ.

9For in him all the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily,

10and in him you are made full, who is the head of all principality and power;

11in whom you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made with hands, in the putting off of the body of the sins of the flesh, in the circumcision of Christ;

12having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith in the working of God, who raised him from the dead.

13You were dead through your trespasses and the uncircumcision of your flesh. He made you alive together with him, having forgiven us all our trespasses,

Three moves in one paragraph. Philosophy 'after the tradition of men' is to be tested, as C08 tests every teacher. And the record against us is not argued down but cancelled, 'nailing it to the cross,' which is the most concrete image in the Pauline letters of C19's open door.

Colossians 2:16-19

A shadow of the things to come; but the body is Christ's

16Let no one therefore judge you in eating, or in drinking, or with respect to a feast day or a new moon or a Sabbath day,

17which are a shadow of the things to come; but the body is Christ’s.

18Let no one rob you of your prize by a voluntary humility and worshipping of the angels, dwelling in the things which he has not seen, vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind,

19and not holding firmly to the Head, from whom all the body, being supplied and knit together through the joints and ligaments, grows with God’s growth.

Feasts, new moons, and sabbaths are called 'a shadow of the things to come' — the same interpretive key C06 takes from Hebrews 10:1, where a shadow implies something casting it. The body, by contrast, 'grows with God's growth,' supplied and knit together from the Head: growth that is received rather than manufactured, C25's picture.

Colossians 2:20-23

Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch

20If you died with Christ from the elements of the world, why, as though living in the world, do you subject yourselves to ordinances,

21“Don’t handle, nor taste, nor touch”

22(all of which perish with use), according to the precepts and doctrines of men?

23Which things indeed appear like wisdom in self-imposed worship, and humility, and severity to the body; but aren’t of any value against the indulgence of the flesh.

Ascetic rules are conceded their appearance — 'like wisdom… and humility, and severity to the body' — and denied their effect: they 'aren't of any value against the indulgence of the flesh.' This is the strongest scriptural statement of the claim C26 and C09 both make, that suppressing a desire does not change it and that holiness comes by acquiring a new want rather than policing the old one. PR20 shares the subject of religious severity but is defined by a guest's arrival, which is absent here.

Colossians 3:1-4

Your life is hidden with Christ in God

3For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.

C23 cites 3:3 to make the point that hiddenness is not a limitation of the arrangement but the arrangement itself. Colossians 3:5-11

Put off the old man… where there can't be Greek and Jew

5Put to death therefore your members which are on the earth: sexual immorality, uncleanness, depraved passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry;

6for which things’ sake the wrath of God comes on the children of disobedience.

7You also once walked in those, when you lived in them;

8but now you also put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and shameful speaking out of your mouth.

9Don’t lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old man with his doings,

10and have put on the new man, who is being renewed in knowledge after the image of his Creator,

11where there can’t be Greek and Jew, circumcision and uncircumcision, barbarian, Scythian, bondservant, freeman; but Christ is all, and in all.

Two lists of what is put to death and put away — the desires, and then the sins of speech: anger, malice, slander, lying — and a new self 'being renewed in knowledge after the image of his Creator.' Renewal as a continuing process, C26's. The renewal is then described as erasing every line by which people rank each other, down to the Scythian, the byword for the barbarian: C27's circle with no one standing outside. PR15 and PR24 were considered. Both share the image; neither is prescribed — the passage states the unity of the new humanity as a fact rather than commanding the widening or the walk toward the centre.

Colossians 3:12-15

Bearing with one another, and forgiving each other

12Put on therefore, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, a heart of compassion, kindness, lowliness, humility, and perseverance;

13bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, if any man has a complaint against any; even as Christ forgave you, so you also do.

14Above all these things, walk in love, which is the bond of perfection.

15And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to which also you were called in one body; and be thankful.

Virtues to be put on like clothing, then their use in friction: bearing with one another and forgiving 'if any man has a complaint against any,' as Christ forgave. The peace of God is to 'rule' in the heart — the verb is the umpire's — because they were called into one body: conflict aimed at the body's repair, which is the Architecture of Peace. Love as 'the bond of perfection' is C29's fully formed heart. PR27 is the nearest it has yet come — a list of virtues and a friction in view — but the passage prescribes the response to the friction, not the practice of reading each friction as the material for a particular virtue, and it is not tagged.

The scope is everything — 'whatever you do, in word or in deed' — and the instruction is to do it in the name of the Lord, with thanks. Colossians 3:18-21

Fathers, don't provoke your children, so that they won't be discouraged

18Wives, be in subjection to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord.

19Husbands, love your wives, and don’t be bitter against them.

20Children, obey your parents in all things, for this pleases the Lord.

21Fathers, don’t provoke your children, so that they won’t be discouraged.

The household code in its shortest form. What is distinctive is that each command to the stronger party is aimed at the weaker party's inner state: husbands are not to be bitter, and fathers are not to provoke their children 'so that they won't be discouraged.' C14's claim that the household is where formation is tested, with the father's conduct measured by what it does to a child's spirit. The instruction to wives sits inside a first-century household code; the index anchors what the passage asks of those who hold power in the home, and the curriculum's guard against loyalty used to keep someone in harm applies.

Colossians 3:22-4:1

Not just when they are looking, as men pleasers

22Servants, obey in all things those who are your masters according to the flesh, not just when they are looking, as men pleasers, but in singleness of heart, fearing God.

23And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord, and not for men,

24knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ.

25But he who does wrong will receive again for the wrong that he has done, and there is no partiality.

1Masters, give to your servants that which is just and equal, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.

Addressed to household slaves, whose situation the index does not commend, the passage names the exact temptation the Hidden Life is for: work done 'when they are looking, as men pleasers.' Against it — whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men. That is PR13's question stated as a command (what would I do if no one would ever find out?) and PR23's claim that the highest form of the road is honest work in the least glamorous setting. Masters are then held to what is 'just and equal' under a Master of their own, which keeps the passage from being read as a charter for the household's power.
