Philippians 1:3-8

He who began a good work in you will complete it

3I thank my God whenever I remember you,

4always in every request of mine on behalf of you all making my requests with joy,

5for your partnership in furtherance of the Good News from the first day until now;

6being confident of this very thing, that he who began a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.

7It is even right for me to think this way on behalf of all of you, because I have you in my heart, because, both in my bonds and in the defense and confirmation of the Good News, you all are partakers with me of grace.

8For God is my witness, how I long after all of you in the tender mercies of Christ Jesus.

Paul's confidence about a community he loves is a confidence about duration: the work was begun and will be completed 'until the day of Jesus Christ,' not by the next letter. He prays for them with joy while the work is visibly unfinished. That is the Discipline of Gradualism applied to other people — refusing to demand immediate perfection of them — and the joy of C10 located in the relationship rather than the result.

Philippians 1:9-11

That your love may abound in knowledge and all discernment

9This I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and all discernment;

10so that you may approve the things that are excellent; that you may be sincere and without offense to the day of Christ;

11being filled with the fruits of righteousness, which are through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

Discernment is asked for as a property of love, not as a check on it: love abounding 'in knowledge and all discernment, so that you may approve the things that are excellent.' C02 makes wisdom a skill in the service of mercy — 'what discernment is for' — and this prayer is that claim in its most compact form.

Philippians 1:12-18

What does it matter? Only that Christ is proclaimed

12Now I desire to have you know, brothers, that the things which happened to me have turned out rather to the progress of the Good News;

13so that it became evident to the whole palace guard, and to all the rest, that my bonds are in Christ;

14and that most of the brothers in the Lord, being confident through my bonds, are more abundantly bold to speak the word of God without fear.

15Some indeed preach Christ even out of envy and strife, and some also out of good will.

16The former insincerely preach Christ from selfish ambition, thinking that they add affliction to my chains;

17but the latter out of love, knowing that I am appointed for the defense of the Good News.

18What does it matter? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed. I rejoice in this, yes, and will rejoice.

Rivals preach 'out of envy and strife,' intending to add to the affliction of a man in chains. He names the motive and declines to answer it: what does it matter, I rejoice. No counter-campaign, no withdrawal of recognition from their work, nothing returned. That is Non-Retaliation in the small, social form the practice warns we barely register. The detachment from his own standing in the matter is C18's, and the absence of grievance where one was invited is C22's measure of a healed heart.

Philippians 1:19-26

To live is Christ, and to die is gain

19For I know that this will turn out to my salvation, through your supplication and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ,

20according to my earnest expectation and hope, that I will in no way be disappointed, but with all boldness, as always, now also Christ will be magnified in my body, whether by life, or by death.

21For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.

22But if I live on in the flesh, this will bring fruit from my work; yet I don’t know what I will choose.

23But I am in a dilemma between the two, having the desire to depart and be with Christ, which is far better.

24Yet, to remain in the flesh is more needful for your sake.

25Having this confidence, I know that I will remain, yes, and remain with you all, for your progress and joy in the faith,

26that your rejoicing may abound in Christ Jesus in me through my presence with you again.

He would rather depart, and says so plainly, and then reasons himself toward staying because it is 'more needful for your sake.' Holy detachment that does not become indifference to others: the freedom of C18 issuing in the preference for the other's good that C16 calls the logic of service. PR26 was considered and declined; he is not free to walk away from his imprisonment, and the passage turns on a choice between life and death, not between staying and leaving a community.

Philippians 1:27-30

Let your way of life be worthy of the Good News

27Only let your way of life be worthy of the Good News of Christ, that, whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear of your state, that you stand firm in one spirit, with one soul striving for the faith of the Good News;

28and in nothing frightened by the adversaries, which is for them a proof of destruction, but to you of salvation, and that from God.

29Because it has been granted to you on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in him, but also to suffer on his behalf,

30having the same conflict which you saw in me, and now hear is in me.

The verb translated 'let your way of life be' is the verb for living as a citizen, and the conduct asked for is public — standing firm in one spirit, unfrightened by opponents. C17's posture of a community whose conduct is visible to those against it, held together by the unity C15 builds.

Philippians 2:1-4

Each counting others better than himself

1If there is therefore any exhortation in Christ, if any consolation of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any tender mercies and compassion,

2make my joy full, by being like-minded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind;

3doing nothing through rivalry or through conceit, but in humility, each counting others better than himself;

4each of you not just looking to his own things, but each of you also to the things of others.

C15 quotes 2:3-4 as the mechanism of the Architecture of Peace, and glosses it as an instruction about attention rather than a request to believe something false: in any dispute, weight the other person's interests as heavily as your own. Rivalry and conceit are named as what peace is built against, and looking to the things of others as what builds it.

Philippians 2:5-11

He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant

5Have this in your mind, which was also in Christ Jesus,

6who, existing in the form of God, didn’t consider equality with God a thing to be grasped,

7but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men.

8And being found in human form, he humbled himself, becoming obedient to death, yes, the death of the cross.

9Therefore God also highly exalted him, and gave to him the name which is above every name;

10that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, those on earth, and those under the earth,

11and that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Equality with God not treated as something to be grasped; the form of a servant taken; obedience to the most degrading death available. C16's argument that authority exists for service has its ground here, and the self-emptying that precedes any exaltation is the hidden life's pattern in C23 — faithfulness first, any vindication left to God. PR25 is not tagged. The hymn is the supreme status reversal in scripture, and status reversal is exactly what has drawn PR25 in error three times before; the practice is giving up one's own seat to the poor, the elderly, or the stranger in a gathering, and nothing here is that.

Philippians 2:12-13

It is God who works in you both to will and to work

12So then, my beloved, even as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.

13For it is God who works in you both to will and to work, for his good pleasure.

Effort and grace are given in a single sentence without either cancelling the other: work out your salvation, for God works in you — and works on the willing as well as the doing. That is C26's mechanism, change by a new want rather than a forced behaviour. The obedience praised as 'much more in my absence' is faithfulness that does not depend on an observer, which C25 needs for a growth too slow to be watched.

Philippians 2:14-18

Without murmurings and disputes, as lights in the world

14Do all things without murmurings and disputes,

Philippians 2:19-30

I have no one else like-minded, who will truly care about you

19But I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, that I also may be cheered up when I know how you are doing.

20For I have no one else like-minded, who will truly care about you.

21For they all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ.

22But you know the proof of him, that, as a child serves a father, so he served with me in furtherance of the Good News.

23Therefore I hope to send him at once, as soon as I see how it will go with me.

24But I trust in the Lord that I myself also will come shortly.

25But I counted it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother, fellow worker, fellow soldier, and your apostle and servant of my need;

26since he longed for you all, and was very troubled, because you had heard that he was sick.

27For indeed he was sick, nearly to death, but God had mercy on him; and not on him only, but on me also, that I might not have sorrow on sorrow.

28I have sent him therefore the more diligently, that, when you see him again, you may rejoice, and that I may be the less sorrowful.

29Receive him therefore in the Lord with all joy, and hold such in honor,

30because for the work of Christ he came near to death, risking his life to supply that which was lacking in your service toward me.

Two men commended, and both for the same thing. Timothy is set against those who 'all seek their own,' and the Philippians 'know the proof of him' — his character tested by service. Epaphroditus came near to death supplying what was lacking. C12 asks for proof of the inner life in what it does for others; C16 makes service the measure of anyone's standing. Both are given here by name.

Philippians 3:1-11

What things were gain to me, these have I counted loss

1Finally, my brothers, rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you, to me indeed is not tiresome, but for you it is safe.

2Beware of the dogs, beware of the evil workers, beware of the false circumcision.

3For we are the circumcision, who worship God in the Spirit, and rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh;

4though I myself might have confidence even in the flesh. If any other man thinks that he has confidence in the flesh, I yet more:

5circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; concerning the law, a Pharisee;

6concerning zeal, persecuting the assembly; concerning the righteousness which is in the law, found blameless.

7However, what things were gain to me, these have I counted loss for Christ.

8Yes most certainly, and I count all things to be loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus, my Lord, for whom I suffered the loss of all things, and count them nothing but refuse, that I may gain Christ

9and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own, that which is of the law, but that which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness which is from God by faith;

10that I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, becoming conformed to his death;

11if by any means I may attain to the resurrection from the dead.

'We are the circumcision, who worship God in the Spirit': the outward sign read inwardly, the move C06 takes from Jeremiah 4:4. Then the inventory of every credential he holds, each one real, counted as loss — not because they were false but because they were not the thing. Detachment in C18's sense, from goods that are genuinely goods.

Philippians 3:12-16

Not that I have already obtained; I press on

12Not that I have already obtained, or am already made perfect; but I press on, if it is so that I may take hold of that for which also I was taken hold of by Christ Jesus.

13Brothers, I don’t regard myself as yet having taken hold, but one thing I do. Forgetting the things which are behind, and stretching forward to the things which are before,

14I press on toward the goal for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.

15Let us therefore, as many as are perfect, think this way. If in anything you think otherwise, God will also reveal that to you.

16Nevertheless, to the extent that we have already attained, let us walk by the same rule. Let us be of the same mind.

A man at the end of his work declares himself unfinished and gives the rule for the unfinished: forget what is behind, stretch toward what is ahead, and 'to the extent that we have already attained, let us walk by the same rule.' That is the Discipline of Gradualism's only question — not am I perfect but am I still walking — prescribed by example and then by command. Forgetting what is behind shares a subject with the Failure Recovery Protocol's refusal to relitigate, but the passage is about achievement, not failure, so PR11 is not tagged.

Philippians 3:17-21

I tell you even weeping; our citizenship is in heaven

17Brothers, be imitators together of me, and note those who walk this way, even as you have us for an example.

18For many walk, of whom I told you often, and now tell you even weeping, as the enemies of the cross of Christ,

19whose end is destruction, whose god is the belly, and whose glory is in their shame, who think about earthly things.

20For our citizenship is in heaven, from where we also wait for a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ;

21who will change the body of our humiliation to be conformed to the body of his glory, according to the working by which he is able even to subject all things to himself.

Two claims frame the paragraph: imitate me, which is C05's apprenticeship, and our citizenship is in heaven, which is C17's anchoring elsewhere. Between them Paul speaks of those whose end is destruction, and he speaks of them weeping. The test Shedding Harshness sets is the movement in the chest at news of someone's downfall; here it is grief, written into the letter, about people he regards as enemies of the cross. PR10 is not tagged: the passage gives the anchoring elsewhere but not the full participation that completes the practice.

Philippians 4:1-3

I exhort Euodia, and I exhort Syntyche

1Therefore, my brothers, beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand firm in the Lord, my beloved.

2I exhort Euodia, and I exhort Syntyche, to think the same way in the Lord.

3Yes, I beg you also, true yokefellow, help these women, for they labored with me in the Good News, with Clement also, and the rest of my fellow workers, whose names are in the book of life.

Two women in the congregation are at odds. Paul addresses each by name with the same verb, assigns no fault, recalls that both laboured beside him, and asks a third person to help them reach agreement. The objective is plainly repair, not a ruling on who was right — the Architecture of Peace in a single live case.

Philippians 4:4-7

The peace of God will guard your hearts

4Rejoice in the Lord always! Again I will say, “Rejoice!”

5Let your gentleness be known to all men. The Lord is at hand.

6In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.

7And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

Rejoicing commanded twice, gentleness made known to all, and anxiety not suppressed but converted into petition with thanksgiving. The promise is a garrison: the verb for 'guard' is the military one, and what is guarded is 'your hearts and your thoughts,' the territory of C20's watchman. The joy is C10's and the unanxious quiet C22's.

Whatever things are true… think about these things

Philippians 4:10-13

I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content

10But I rejoice in the Lord greatly, that now at length you have revived your thought for me; in which you did indeed take thought, but you lacked opportunity.

11Not that I speak in respect to lack, for I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content in it.

12I know how to be humbled, and I know also how to abound. In everything and in all things I have learned the secret both to be filled and to be hungry, both to abound and to be in need.

13I can do all things through Christ, who strengthens me.

Contentment is described as a thing learned — 'I have learned the secret' — across both abundance and need, which means it is a skill rather than a temperament. That is C18's detachment in its mature form: not the absence of goods but freedom with respect to their presence or absence. The verse usually quoted alone, 'I can do all things,' is about this, not about achievement.

Philippians 4:14-20

You sent once and again to my need

14However you did well that you shared in my affliction.

15You yourselves also know, you Philippians, that in the beginning of the Good News, when I departed from Macedonia, no assembly shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving but you only.

16For even in Thessalonica you sent once and again to my need.

17Not that I seek for the gift, but I seek for the fruit that increases to your account.

18But I have all things, and abound. I am filled, having received from Epaphroditus the things that came from you, a sweet-smelling fragrance, an acceptable and well-pleasing sacrifice to God.

19My God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus.

20Now to our God and Father be the glory forever and ever! Amen.

The Philippians alone shared with him 'in the matter of giving and receiving,' and they did it repeatedly — 'once and again.' Giving as a rhythm rather than an impulse is Habitual Almsgiving's definition. Paul's reply refuses to make the gift about his own need and calls it fruit increasing to their account and a sacrifice acceptable to God: the proof C12 asks for. PR28 is not tagged; nothing indicates surplus redirected from expansion.
