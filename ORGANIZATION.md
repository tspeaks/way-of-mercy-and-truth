# Organization: What Is Free and What Is Not

The Way of Mercy and Truth has two halves that must stay structurally separate. This
document says which is which, and why the separation matters.

## The split

| | **Free layer** | **Commercial layer** |
|---|---|---|
| **Contents** | The curriculum, the genealogy, the commons materials | Print and audio editions; applied stewardship practice; commissioned work |
| **Where** | This repository | Separate entities, separate repositories |
| **License** | CC0 — public domain, no attribution required | Ordinary commercial terms |
| **Price** | Free, permanently | Priced |
| **Purpose** | Formation | Livelihood |

## The rule

**Nothing in the free layer is ever paywalled, gated, upsold, or used as a funnel.**

No part of the curriculum is held back as premium content. There is no "advanced course."
There is no members-only tier. A person who never pays anything has the entire Way, in
full, forever.

If the free layer ever starts functioning as marketing for the paid layer, the split has
failed and the free layer has been corrupted.

## What may be sold

**Print and audio editions.** The text is free; a physical book, a produced audiobook, or
a bound edition is a manufactured object with real costs. Selling one does not restrict
anyone's access to the other. Anyone else may also print and sell it — CC0 permits that,
and that is fine.

**Applied stewardship practice.** Consulting, advising, teaching, or workshop facilitation
is professional labor and may be charged for. What is being sold is *time and judgment*,
never access to the material.

**Commissioned work.** Custom curricula, translations, or adaptations built for a specific
organization.

## What must never be sold

- Access to the curriculum, in any form or portion
- Membership in a company or the Commons
- Certification, licensing, or accreditation to teach the Way
- Rights to the material itself — CC0 has already given those away irrevocably

That fourth item is worth stating plainly: the dedication cannot be walked back. Nobody,
including the author, can later restrict what has already been released.

## Why the separation

Three reasons, in order of importance.

**The curriculum cannot be trusted if it is a sales instrument.** A book that tells you to
practice humility and non-retaliation, and which exists to generate leads, is not credible
about either. The reader has to be able to take it at face value, and they can only do
that if there is nothing behind it.

**The Commons cannot be a customer base.** A company meeting to make honest report about
where they are failing is doing something that does not survive being marketed to. If
groups form and their members become a pipeline, the confession stops.

**Money moving through a formation project distorts it.** Not necessarily through bad
faith — more often through slow drift toward whatever converts. Keeping the free layer
structurally incapable of generating revenue removes the pressure entirely.

## Practical boundaries

- Applied stewardship work operates under its own name and its own materials, and does not
  require clients to adopt the theological framework.
- The Commons has no dues, no fees, and no paid tier.
- This repository accepts no sponsorship that would create an expectation about content.
- Where the applied work draws on the curriculum, it cites it as the free public resource
  it is.

## A note on the author's situation

This split is not a claim to be above needing money. It is the opposite: it is a
recognition that the pressure is real, and that the way to keep it from bending the work
is to build a structure where it cannot.

The curriculum is not the business plan. It was never meant to be.
