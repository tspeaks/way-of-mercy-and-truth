# 1 Peter — review package

Scripture: World English Bible (public domain). Index status: complete — 100 of 105 verses; the epistolary greeting (1:1-2) and closing (5:12-14) are unindexed; awaiting second-pass review. Method version 2.3.

Every entry below shows the passage in full, then the claim made about it. Read the passage before the claim. The question is not whether the tags are internally consistent — it is whether they survive contact with the text.

**Note.** A letter to foreigners in the Dispersion, and the text the curriculum leans on for C17. Three entries carry prior citations from C14, C17, and C23, which serve as the calibration set. The household instructions in 2:18-3:7 are indexed for what they anchor, with the curriculum's own guard against loyalty that is used to keep someone in harm stated in the entries rather than left implicit.

**Prediction note.** The indexing order named 1 Peter 'the core text for C17, the Citizen-Stranger.' Five entries reach C17 (1:17-21, 2:11-12, 2:13-17, 3:13-17, 4:1-6) and two carry PR10. Whether that amounts to the core text or merely a strong one is left to the reviewer; the drafter wrote the prediction and should not grade it.

**Coverage note.** The greeting (1:1-2) and closing (5:12-14) are left out as in James. The greeting's address — 'foreigners in the Dispersion' — frames the whole letter and is picked up where the letter turns it into an instruction, at 1:17 and 2:11. No entry reaches PR24, PR27, or PR28, the three practices still unanchored anywhere in the index; 2:4-10 builds a house out of living stones, which shares an image with Virtue Construction and does none of what PR27 describes.

## 1. 1 Peter 1:3-9 — Though now you don't see him, you rejoice

> **1:3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to his great mercy became our father again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,
> **1:4** to an incorruptible and undefiled inheritance that doesn’t fade away, reserved in Heaven for you,
> **1:5** who by the power of God are guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.
> **1:6** Wherein you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while, if need be, you have been put to grief in various trials,
> **1:7** that the proof of your faith, which is more precious than gold that perishes even though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise, glory, and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ—
> **1:8** whom not having known you love; in whom, though now you don’t see him, yet believing, you rejoice greatly with joy unspeakable and full of glory—
> **1:9** receiving the result of your faith, the salvation of your souls.

**Claim.** The letter opens on hope before it says a word about conduct, and the hope is set inside grief rather than after it: 'for a little while… you have been put to grief in various trials.' Joy is attributed to people who cannot see what they love. C19 begins with hope deliberately for the same reason, and C10 makes the same claim that joy is a consequence of where one stands, not of what one currently feels.

## 2. 1 Peter 1:10-12 — The prophets searched; angels desire to look

> **1:10** Concerning this salvation, the prophets sought and searched diligently, who prophesied of the grace that would come to you,
> **1:11** searching for who or what kind of time the Spirit of Christ, which was in them, pointed to, when he predicted the sufferings of Christ, and the glories that would follow them.
> **1:12** To them it was revealed, that not to themselves, but to you, they ministered these things, which now have been announced to you through those who preached the Good News to you by the Holy Spirit sent out from heaven; which things angels desire to look into.

**Claim.** The prophets are described as ministering a message whose time and subject they were still searching for — 'not to themselves, but to you.' This is the reading habit C06 teaches: the older text points past itself, and its first speakers did not hold the whole of what they carried. It shares subject matter with the Discipline of Unknowing but prescribes no posture to the reader, so PR04 is not tagged.

## 3. 1 Peter 1:13-16 — Prepare your minds for action; be sober

> **1:13** Therefore, prepare your minds for action, be sober and set your hope fully on the grace that will be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ—
> **1:14** as children of obedience, not conforming yourselves according to your former lusts as in your ignorance,
> **1:15** but just as he who called you is holy, you yourselves also be holy in all of your behavior;
> **1:16** because it is written, “You shall be holy; for I am holy.”

**Claim.** 'Be sober' is the verb from which the desert writers took nepsis, the name of the practice itself, and here it is joined to a specific object: 'not conforming yourselves according to your former lusts.' Readiness of mind against the old desires before they are acted on is Watchfulness in its source form. The holiness command that follows gives the reason for the attention, not a separate instruction.

## 4. 1 Peter 1:17-21 — Pass the time of your living as foreigners here

> **1:17** If you call on him as Father, who without respect of persons judges according to each man’s work, pass the time of your living as foreigners here in reverent fear:
> **1:18** knowing that you were redeemed, not with corruptible things, with silver or gold, from the useless way of life handed down from your fathers,
> **1:19** but with precious blood, as of a faultless and pure lamb, the blood of Christ;
> **1:20** who was foreknown indeed before the foundation of the world, but was revealed at the end of times for your sake,
> **1:21** who through him are believers in God, who raised him from the dead, and gave him glory; so that your faith and hope might be in God.

**Claim.** The address of the letter — foreigners in the Dispersion — becomes an instruction: live out the stay as foreigners, in reverent fear. The ground given is a redemption 'not with corruptible things, with silver or gold, from the useless way of life handed down from your fathers.' Residence without ultimate belonging (C17) and the loosening of what silver and gold can purchase (C18) are given one argument.

## 5. 1 Peter 1:22-25 — All flesh is like grass; the word remains forever

> **1:22** Seeing you have purified your souls in your obedience to the truth through the Spirit in sincere brotherly affection, love one another from the heart fervently:
> **1:23** having been born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, through the word of God, which lives and remains forever.
> **1:24** For, “All flesh is like grass, and all of man’s glory like the flower in the grass. The grass withers, and its flower falls;
> **1:25** but the Lord’s word endures forever.” This is the word of Good News which was preached to you.

**Claim.** Purification is measured by its outcome, 'sincere brotherly affection,' and the command is to love 'from the heart fervently' — the inner work proved by what it produces between people, which is the argument of C12. The Isaiah quotation sets that love against the transience of all human glory, which is the comparison C18 makes when it asks what is worth holding.

## 6. 1 Peter 2:1-3 — As newborn babies, long for the pure milk

> **2:1** Putting away therefore all wickedness, all deceit, hypocrisies, envies, and all evil speaking,
> **2:2** as newborn babies, long for the pure milk of the Word, that you may grow thereby,
> **2:3** if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is gracious:

**Claim.** The list of things to put away — deceit, hypocrisy, envy, evil speaking — is the inventory of a soul in friction with others, the territory of C22. The image that follows is of an infant feeding 'that you may grow thereby': growth by nourishment rather than by effort, at the pace of a newborn. That is C25's picture, though the passage commands the longing rather than the patience, so PR14 is not tagged.

## 7. 1 Peter 2:4-10 — Who had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy

> **2:4** coming to him, a living stone, rejected indeed by men, but chosen by God, precious.
> **2:5** You also, as living stones, are built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.
> **2:6** Because it is contained in Scripture, “Behold, I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone, chosen, and precious: He who believes in him will not be disappointed.”
> **2:7** For you who believe therefore is the honor, but for those who are disobedient, “The stone which the builders rejected, has become the chief cornerstone,”
> **2:8** and, “a stone of stumbling, and a rock of offense.” For they stumble at the word, being disobedient, to which also they were appointed.
> **2:9** But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God’s own possession, that you may proclaim the excellence of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light:
> **2:10** who in time past were no people, but now are God’s people, who had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.

**Claim.** People rejected or of no account — 'no people' — are built as living stones into a house for God, the same stone the builders rejected becoming the cornerstone. C26's temple is built out of material that had no business carrying light. The closing line names the change as mercy received, and C19's door is the same: those who had not obtained mercy now have.

## 8. 1 Peter 2:11-12 — Foreigners and pilgrims, by your good works which they see

> **2:11** Beloved, I beg you as foreigners and pilgrims, to abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul;
> **2:12** having good behavior among the nations, so in that of which they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they see, glorify God in the day of visitation.

**Claim.** C17 quotes these two verses in full as the pattern of the chapter. The mechanism is neither argument nor power but conduct observed by people who are speaking against the community — full participation in a society that suspects you, while the loyalty that shapes the conduct is held elsewhere. That is the Citizen-Stranger's definition stated as a command.

## 9. 1 Peter 2:13-17 — As free, and not using your freedom for a cloak

> **2:13** Therefore subject yourselves to every ordinance of man for the Lord’s sake: whether to the king, as supreme;
> **2:14** or to governors, as sent by him for vengeance on evildoers and for praise to those who do well.
> **2:15** For this is the will of God, that by well-doing you should put to silence the ignorance of foolish men:
> **2:16** as free, and not using your freedom for a cloak of wickedness, but as bondservants of God.
> **2:17** Honor all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the king.

**Claim.** Submission to human ordinance is grounded 'for the Lord's sake,' and immediately qualified: 'as free… as bondservants of God.' Obeying the laws gladly while the final loyalty rests above any decree is PR10's own wording of the practice. 'Honor all men' closes the paragraph, which is the practice's refusal of contempt stated without exception.

## 10. 1 Peter 2:18-25 — When he was cursed, he didn't curse back

> **2:18** Servants, be in subjection to your masters with all fear; not only to the good and gentle, but also to the wicked.
> **2:19** For it is commendable if someone endures pain, suffering unjustly, because of conscience toward God.
> **2:20** For what glory is it if, when you sin, you patiently endure beating? But if, when you do well, you patiently endure suffering, this is commendable with God.
> **2:21** For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his steps,
> **2:22** who did not sin, “neither was deceit found in his mouth.”
> **2:23** Who, when he was cursed, didn’t curse back. When he suffered, didn’t threaten, but committed himself to him who judges righteously;
> **2:24** who his own self bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live to righteousness; by whose stripes you were healed.
> **2:25** For you were going astray like sheep; but now have returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.

**Claim.** The instruction is addressed to household slaves with no legal exit from harsh masters, and the index does not lift it into counsel to remain under abuse; the same guard the curriculum attaches to PR26 applies here. What it anchors is the pattern in 2:21-23, offered as the example to follow: cursed, he did not curse back; suffering, he did not threaten, but handed judgment to God. That is Non-Retaliation in action and in speech, with its interior mechanism named. The closing image, sheep returned to the Shepherd, is the image C19 opens with.

## 11. 1 Peter 3:1-6 — The hidden person of the heart

> **3:1** In the same way, wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; so that, even if any don’t obey the Word, they may be won by the behavior of their wives without a word;
> **3:2** seeing your pure behavior in fear.
> **3:3** Let your beauty be not just the outward adorning of braiding the hair, and of wearing jewels of gold, or of putting on fine clothing;
> **3:4** but in the hidden person of the heart, in the incorruptible adornment of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God very precious.
> **3:5** For this is how the holy women before, who hoped in God also adorned themselves, being in subjection to their own husbands:
> **3:6** as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, whose children you now are, if you do well, and are not put in fear by any terror.

**Claim.** C23 cites 3:4 directly: value located in the hidden person of the heart, the incorruptible adornment of a gentle and quiet spirit, precious in the sight of God. The surrounding verses describe a conduct that wins 'without a word' — the same mechanism as 2:12, made unwitnessed and unargued. The instruction to wives sits inside a first-century household code; the index anchors the location of value in the unseen, not the code's ordering of the household, and the PR26 guard applies here as well.

## 12. 1 Peter 3:7 — Joint heirs of the grace of life

> **3:7** You husbands, in the same way, live with your wives according to knowledge, giving honor to the woman, as to the weaker vessel, as being also joint heirs of the grace of life; that your prayers may not be hindered.

**Claim.** The husband is told to live with his wife 'according to knowledge,' giving honour, as a co-heir rather than a subordinate, and the reason given is that his prayers may otherwise be hindered. C14's claim is that the household is where formation is tested, and this verse makes domestic conduct a condition of the interior life rather than a separate department of it.

## 13. 1 Peter 3:8-12 — Not rendering evil for evil, or insult for insult, but blessing

> **3:8** Finally, be all like-minded, compassionate, loving as brothers, tenderhearted, courteous,
> **3:9** not rendering evil for evil, or insult for insult; but instead blessing; knowing that to this were you called, that you may inherit a blessing.
> **3:10** For, “He who would love life, and see good days, let him keep his tongue from evil, and his lips from speaking deceit.
> **3:11** Let him turn away from evil, and do good. Let him seek peace, and pursue it.
> **3:12** For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, and his ears open to their prayer; but the face of the Lord is against those who do evil.”

**Claim.** The plainest statement of Non-Retaliation outside the Gospels, covering both action and speech, with blessing returned in place of the blow. Peter then quotes Psalm 34:12-16, the same psalm C15 takes as its witness, and from it the command to 'seek peace, and pursue it' — peace as something actively built toward, which is the Architecture of Peace aiming at repair rather than victory.

## 14. 1 Peter 3:13-17 — Ready to give an answer, with humility and fear

> **3:13** Now who is he who will harm you, if you become imitators of that which is good?
> **3:14** But even if you should suffer for righteousness’ sake, you are blessed. “Don’t fear what they fear, neither be troubled.”
> **3:15** But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts; and always be ready to give an answer to everyone who asks you a reason concerning the hope that is in you, with humility and fear:
> **3:16** having a good conscience; that, while you are spoken against as evildoers, they may be disappointed who curse your good way of life in Christ.
> **3:17** For it is better, if it is God’s will, that you suffer for doing well than for doing evil.

**Claim.** The defence of hope is to be given to anyone who asks, but its manner is specified — humility, fear, a good conscience — so that those who slander are put to shame by the life rather than by the argument. C17's public-square posture. The practice tag is carried by 2:11-12 and 2:13-17, which state it more directly; repeating it here would add a count rather than an anchor.

## 15. 1 Peter 3:18-22 — The righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you to God

> **3:18** Because Christ also suffered for sins once, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring you to God; being put to death in the flesh, but made alive in the spirit;
> **3:19** in which he also went and preached to the spirits in prison,
> **3:20** who before were disobedient, when God waited patiently in the days of Noah, while the ship was being built. In it, few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.
> **3:21** This is a symbol of baptism, which now saves you—not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,
> **3:22** who is at the right hand of God, having gone into heaven, angels and authorities and powers being made subject to him.

**Claim.** The centre of the paragraph is the purpose clause — the righteous for the unrighteous, 'that he might bring you to God' — which is C19's door. Baptism is then defined against its outward reading: not the washing of the flesh but the answer of a good conscience toward God, the inward reading of an outward sign that C06 teaches. The preaching to the spirits in prison is among the most disputed verses in the New Testament and is left uninterpreted here.

## 16. 1 Peter 4:1-6 — They think it is strange that you don't run with them

> **4:1** Forasmuch then as Christ suffered for us in the flesh, arm yourselves also with the same mind; for he who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin;
> **4:2** that you no longer should live the rest of your time in the flesh for the lusts of men, but for the will of God.
> **4:3** For we have spent enough of our past time doing the desire of the Gentiles, and having walked in lewdness, lusts, drunken binges, orgies, carousings, and abominable idolatries.
> **4:4** They think it is strange that you don’t run with them into the same excess of riot, blaspheming:
> **4:5** who will give account to him who is ready to judge the living and the dead.
> **4:6** For to this end the Good News was preached even to the dead, that they might be judged indeed as men in the flesh, but live as to God in the spirit.

**Claim.** The cost of the citizen-stranger's posture is described from the other side: former companions find it strange and speak against it. The instruction to 'arm yourselves also with the same mind' and live the rest of one's time for the will of God rather than the desires of men is a resolve made in advance against a known pull, which is the forward attention of C20.

## 17. 1 Peter 4:7-11 — Be hospitable to one another without grumbling

> **4:7** But the end of all things is near. Therefore be of sound mind, self-controlled, and sober in prayer.
> **4:8** And above all things be earnest in your love among yourselves, for love covers a multitude of sins.
> **4:9** Be hospitable to one another without grumbling.
> **4:10** As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another, as good managers of the grace of God in its various forms.
> **4:11** If anyone speaks, let it be as it were the very words of God. If anyone serves, let it be as of the strength which God supplies, that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom belong the glory and the dominion forever and ever. Amen.

**Claim.** Hospitality is commanded with its characteristic failure named — grumbling — and gifts are to be used 'as good managers of the grace of God,' held for others rather than owned. C16's service and C14's household open door. Two tags were considered and declined. 'Love covers a multitude of sins' shares PR17's subject but describes what love does, not the act of shielding someone from exposure. Hospitality without grumbling shares PR20's subject, but nothing here breaks a routine for a guest; the grumbling is the host's, not a peevish guest's, so PR22 does not apply either.

## 18. 1 Peter 4:12-19 — Don't be astonished at the fiery trial

> **4:12** Beloved, don’t be astonished at the fiery trial which has come upon you, to test you, as though a strange thing happened to you.
> **4:13** But because you are partakers of Christ’s sufferings, rejoice; that at the revelation of his glory you also may rejoice with exceeding joy.
> **4:14** If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed; because the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you. On their part he is blasphemed, but on your part he is glorified.
> **4:15** For let none of you suffer as a murderer, or a thief, or an evil doer, or a meddler in other men’s matters.
> **4:16** But if one of you suffers for being a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God in this matter.
> **4:17** For the time has come for judgment to begin with the household of God. If it begins first with us, what will happen to those who don’t obey the Good News of God?
> **4:18** “If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will happen to the ungodly and the sinner?”
> **4:19** Therefore let them also who suffer according to the will of God in doing good entrust their souls to him, as to a faithful Creator.

**Claim.** C14 cites 4:19 directly, and the Scriptural Appendix names this passage as the ground of Unreciprocated Care: keep doing good while it hurts and entrust the soul to a faithful Creator. The drafter notes the attractor test without overriding the author: the passage is about being insulted for doing good, and PR22 is defined more narrowly as bearing with ungrateful behaviour from those one is helping. The overlap is real — doing good to people who answer it with insult — but the recipients of the good are not named as the ones insulting. The author's placement stands; a reviewer who disagrees is disagreeing with WMT A3.

## 19. 1 Peter 5:1-4 — Not lording it over those entrusted to you

> **5:1** I exhort the elders among you, as a fellow elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and who will also share in the glory that will be revealed.
> **5:2** Shepherd the flock of God which is among you, exercising the oversight, not under compulsion, but voluntarily, not for dishonest gain, but willingly;
> **5:3** neither as lording it over those entrusted to you, but making yourselves examples to the flock.
> **5:4** When the chief Shepherd is revealed, you will receive the crown of glory that doesn’t fade away.

**Claim.** Three paired contrasts for those who hold authority — not under compulsion but willingly, not for gain but eagerly, not lording it but as examples — written by a man who calls himself a fellow elder rather than a superior. C16 opens by saying authority corrupts nearly everyone who holds it; this is the apostolic rule against each of the three corruptions.

## 20. 1 Peter 5:5-7 — Clothe yourselves with humility; cast all your worries on him

> **5:5** Likewise, you younger ones, be subject to the elder. Yes, all of you clothe yourselves with humility, to subject yourselves to one another; for “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”
> **5:6** Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time;
> **5:7** casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.

**Claim.** Humility is commanded toward one another before it is commanded toward God, and it is pictured as something put on — the verb is the one for tying on a servant's apron. The anxiety that follows is not suppressed but handed over 'because he cares for you,' which is C18's freedom from what one is grasping, and the posture of the weaned child in C22's psalm.

## 21. 1 Peter 5:8-11 — Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful

> **5:8** Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful. Your adversary, the devil, walks around like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.
> **5:9** Withstand him steadfast in your faith, knowing that your brothers who are in the world are undergoing the same sufferings.
> **5:10** But may the God of all grace, who called you to his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after you have suffered a little while, perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you.
> **5:11** To him be the glory and the power forever and ever. Amen.

**Claim.** The two verbs of the desert watchman in one line — be sober, be watchful — with the danger pictured as something circling rather than something already inside. The counsel is steadiness ('withstand him steadfast') and solidarity with others under the same pressure, not anxious self-scrutiny, which is the tone C20 insists on. The promise that follows is restoration after 'a little while,' not exemption.
