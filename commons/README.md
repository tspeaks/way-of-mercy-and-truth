# The Christian Commons

The book already specifies what this is:

> Small companies of people meeting somewhere and doing the plain work of mercy in their
> own streets... reporting honestly, each week, on what they attempted and how it went.

That is the whole design. It is not a movement, a platform, or an institution. It is
groups of people walking the same road and telling the truth about how it is going.

## What a company is

Three to twelve people. Meeting weekly or fortnightly, in person where possible.

Working through the curriculum at **one chapter per week**, holding **two practices**
between them for a year before adding a third.

**The meeting has one job: honest report.** Not discussion.

> The reflection questions attached to each practice are more valuable than the
> discussion questions you will be tempted to invent. Discussion produces opinions. The
> reflection questions produce confession, which is the point.

## A meeting

45–60 minutes. No preparation beyond having read the chapter and attempted the practices.

1. **Read** the week's scripture aloud. (5 min)
2. **Report.** Each person answers, on their two practices: *What did I attempt? Where
   did I fail? What did I notice?* No cross-talk, no fixing, no advice unless asked.
   (30 min)
3. **One reflection question** from the chapter, taken slowly. (15 min)
4. **The blessing** from the end of the chapter, read aloud. (2 min)

If someone reports failure every week, that is not a problem with them. That is the
report, and having the report is worth more than not having it.

## Ground rules

- **Confession, not diagnosis.** You report on yourself. You do not report on anyone else.
- **No fixing.** Advice on request only.
- **What is said here stays here.** Without exception.
- **Attendance is not a virtue.** Return the same day you fail; return to the group the
  next time it meets. No ceremony, no penance.
- **This does not replace church.** A company is not a congregation. If it starts
  functioning as one, disband it.

## Starting one

You need a Bible, a copy of the curriculum, a room, and one other person.

You do not need permission, a platform, a cohort, a launch, or a curriculum license.

> And do not wait for any of it. You do not need a movement, a platform, a cohort, or my
> permission. You need a Bible, a congregation, someone who will tell you the truth about
> yourself, and tomorrow morning.

## Field reports

If your company finds where this fails — a practice that does not work as written, a
stage that is out of order for real people, an instruction that cannot be followed —
that is the most valuable thing you can send back.

Open an issue, or keep it in a branch under `branches/`. Field reports do not go in the
trunk; they are what the trunk gets tested against.
